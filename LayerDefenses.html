KYC and PCI DSS are part of the fraud story.

These are not separate compliance vocabulary lessons. Each control protects a different point in the same risk lifecycle. Select a control to expand it.

Before Approval

KYC verifies the merchant.

Know Your Customer confirms that the business and its owners are who they claim to be before processing begins.

Select to see why it matters +

KYC helps the processor understand who it is extending transaction exposure to. Identity, ownership, and business details are reviewed during underwriting.

At Authorization

Fraud controls evaluate the sale.

EMV, PIN, CVV, AVS, biometrics, and transaction scoring help determine whether a payment appears legitimate.

Select to see why it matters +

Stopping a suspicious transaction before approval is far less expensive than trying to recover funds after fraud has already occurred.

Around Card Data

PCI DSS protects the environment.

PCI DSS establishes the security practices used to protect cardholder data wherever it is stored, processed, or transmitted.

Select to see why it matters +

Secure systems, limited access, encryption, and tokenization reduce the chance that stolen data can be used for future fraud.

After Activation

Monitoring catches change.

Volume, ticket size, refunds, and chargebacks are tracked because a merchant's risk profile can evolve.

Select to see why it matters +

Sudden changes may signal fraud, operational problems, or financial stress. Early intervention is cheaper than account restrictions and losses.

Key Takeaway

The objective is managed risk, not zero risk.

Know who is processing, protect the data, evaluate each transaction, and keep watching what changes.

Know • Protect • Detect • Monitor
