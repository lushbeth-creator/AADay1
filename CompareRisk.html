Debit and credit move risk differently.

Both payment types can be fraudulent or disputed. The difference is where the money comes from, how the customer is authenticated, and where the exposure sits.

CREDIT

Credit extends the promise.

The issuing bank pays on the cardholder's behalf and expects repayment later.

Issuer credit exposure

The issuer takes the risk that the cardholder may not repay.

Chargeback exposure

Fraud, non-delivery, or merchant disputes can reverse funds after settlement.

Approval is conditional

Authorization confirms available credit, not that the sale can never be challenged.

DEBIT

Debit changes the source, not the need for protection.

The transaction draws directly from funds in the customer's bank account.

Less cardholder credit exposure

The customer is using deposited funds rather than borrowing on a credit line.

Fraud still exists

Stolen credentials, account takeover, and card-not-present fraud can still create losses.

Authentication matters

PIN-authenticated debit generally provides stronger evidence than signature or card-not-present debit.
